# Breakup

**A neon brick-breaker played as a roguelite. Four acts of towers drawn in a new
order every run, a perk after every level, a boss at the end of every act — and
after each boss, the choice to cash out or go deeper.**

This is a brick-breaker that won't sit still on one screen. Every level is a
tower — fifteen bricks wide, up to sixty rows tall — and the camera climbs it
with your ball. You are not here to clear the wall. You are here to find the
crystals buried in it, while the tower throws chain explosions, enemies and one
very tempting bad power-up at you.

And no two runs climb the same towers. Every game is a run: the levels come out
of the deck in a different order, bent by mutators, and what you pick up on the
way changes the rules you play the rest of it by.

## Every game is a run

Four acts, one for each world, and every act is three levels and a boss. The
levels are drawn from that world's pool in a new order every time, and from the
second act on they often arrive under a **mutator**: mirrored left to right,
armored a grade tougher, brittle a grade weaker, a gold rush, a swarm of
enemies, or a surge of capsules.

Three lives for the whole run, and every level you finish pays 500 for each one
still in hand — so the run that never dies is worth thousands more than the run
that scrapes through.

At the end of the fourth act is The Sovereign. Beat it and the run is won, with
a victory bonus on top — or keep going, into endless acts drawn from the whole
game, for as long as you last.

## A perk after every level

Clear a level and three perks are on the table. You keep one for the rest of the
run.

There are fourteen, and none of them is a bigger number. Each one is a rule that
hooks into something the game already does, so they stack into each other.
**Crystal Blast** makes crystals explode when they break. **Chain Reaction**
makes every brick an explosion breaks count towards your combo. **Inferno**
widens every explosion while the fireball is lit. Take all three and a tower
starts clearing itself. **Magnet** pulls capsules to your paddle, **Soft Hands**
keeps the combo alive through a caught ball, **Glass Cannon** lifts the combo
cap from 5× to 8× and makes the ball faster to pay for it, and **Meltdown** lets
the fireball through steel.

Eight are there from your first run. The other six are earned — by beating The
Gyre, by detonating eight explosive bricks in one level, by reaching a combo of
5× — and the PERKS page says what each one takes.

## Cash out, or go deeper

Every boss you beat asks the same question.

**Cash out**, and the run ends with every point you have. **Go deeper**, and the
score multiplier goes up — but a curse comes with it for the rest of the run
(faster balls, basic bricks that take two hits, fewer capsules, a shorter paddle,
enemies that come back sooner), and if you die before the next chance to cash
out, you lose half of everything you scored since.

The greedy run and the safe run both get onto the score table. Only one of them
gets to the top of it.

## Break the crystals, not the wall

Every tower hides crystals. Shatter all of them and the level ends, whatever
else is still standing. Everything else in there is either in your way or a
weapon.

- **Steel never breaks.** It isn't an obstacle, it's the shape of the room —
  and in Bastion or Foundry, learning to bank off it _is_ the level.
- **Explosive bricks take their neighbours with them**, and those take theirs.
  One good shot into the right cell of Furnace brings down half a tower you had
  budgeted five minutes for.
- **Gold pays 500 for a single hit.** Tough brick takes three and pays 300.
- **Crystals are 1000 each** — and they are the exit.

## The combo is the real game

Every brick you break before the ball returns to your paddle raises a
multiplier, one step every five bricks, up to 5×. Each link in the chain rings a
semitone higher than the last, so a good run climbs a scale instead of repeating
a noise — and an explosion pays out at whatever multiplier the ball that lit it
had built.

That is the whole scoring game: a tower taken in one long, greedy chain pays
several times what the same tower pays played safe. Everyone at the top of the
score table is someone who stopped touching the ball.

## Nine capsules, and the most common one is a gamble

About one brick in five drops something: expand, laser, catch, extra life,
multiball, slow, fireball, a barrier across your floor — and **shrink**, in the
same capsule, out of the same bricks, and the single most likely thing to fall.

Shrink makes your paddle small and hard to play with. It also puts one more on
the combo multiplier for as long as it lasts. So the capsule drifting down at you
is a decision, and you have to make it while it falls.

## Four kinds of enemy, none of which can kill you

A drifter wanders the field. A diver comes down at your paddle in a serpentine
line. A splitter breaks into two smaller ones when you hit it. A saucer shoots —
and its shot costs you no life, it shrinks your paddle.

What all four really do is stand exactly where your ball was going. You can
laser one (400), hit it with the ball (300), or drive the paddle straight into it
(50). Catch one in an explosion and it leaves a capsule behind.

## Seven bosses, and no two fight the same

Every act ends with one: one of a pair in each of the first three, and The
Sovereign alone at the end of the fourth.

A boss is this game's own vocabulary turned against you: a crystal — the thing
every other level asks you to break — sitting behind a ring of armour plates
that look like bricks and orbit it. A plate takes your hit and gives you
nothing. The crystal is the only thing that counts, and the only way to it is a
gap. Land enough hits and the ring slams shut, faster, throwing out anything
caught inside. Three phases each, and the arena escalates with the boss.

**The Warden** turns one slow ring and shoots what you can see coming. **The
Gyre** spins hard the other way and its plates take three hits. **The Brood**
barely has a ring — it fills the field with enemies instead. **The Binary** puts
a second ring inside the first, turning against it, so a gap is two gaps that
have to line up. **The Pulsar** breathes its ring open and shut and fires in
every direction at once. **The Stalker** comes down the screen after you. **The
Sovereign** does all of it.

## Four worlds, twenty towers

Cyan Dawn, Emerald Drift, Ember Fields, Violet Void — each with its own palette,
its own sky and its own four music tracks, and five hand-designed towers in each
for the run to draw from.

First Steps is a straight and honest wall. The Core is sixty-three rows, three
saucers, and three crystals buried in the middle of it. In between are
Honeycomb, The Vault, Furnace, Meteor Rain, Colossus, Nebula, Dark Lattice,
Stormfront and more, and none of them is the same idea twice.

## The daily run

One run a day, the same one for everybody who plays it that day: the same
towers in the same order, the same mutators, every perk in the game on offer
whether you have unlocked it or not. It keeps a best score of its own, and the whole thing works offline — the seed is
the date.

## Keep the moment

Press **G** and the last eight seconds of play are saved as a GIF in your
Pictures folder. The game is always keeping the last eight seconds, so the
cascade you want to show somebody is already recorded by the time you reach for
the key. (In the downloaded versions; the browser has nowhere to put the file.)

## Controls

- **Mouse or arrow keys** move the paddle — the game follows whichever one you
  touched last.
- **Space or click** serves the ball, fires the lasers, and lets go of a ball
  the paddle has caught.
- **Up, down and Enter** — or **1, 2, 3**, or the mouse — take a perk.
- **Esc** or **P** pauses. **G** saves a clip. **F** is fullscreen, at any
  moment.
- Music and sound effects have their own volumes.

## Play it with the sound on

Half of what this game does, it does with noise: every brick in a chain a
semitone above the last, the crack of a crystal, the jingle when a tower finally
comes down.

The soundtrack is nineteen looping chiptune tracks by **Abstraction** — his
whole public-domain album _Three Red Hearts_ — four per world, two more the boss
fights share, and one for the menu you will hear more of than you expect.

## Free

Free on Windows, macOS and Linux, and playable right here in your browser with
nothing to install. Your settings, unlocked perks and best runs are kept between
sessions either way, and the game never touches the network.

There is a donate button if the evening was worth something to you, and thank
you — but nothing is waiting on it.
